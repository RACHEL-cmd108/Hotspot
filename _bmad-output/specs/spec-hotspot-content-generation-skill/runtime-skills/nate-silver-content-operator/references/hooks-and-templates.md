# Hook and drafting library

Use one hook only. Replace placeholders with verified specifics.

## Hooks

### Counterintuitive claim

`Most people are reading [X] as [A]. The stronger interpretation is probably [B], because [decisive evidence].`

### Concept correction

`People are conflating [A] with [B]. [Observed change] tells us about [A]; it does not yet establish [B].`

### Baseline reversal

`[Number] sounds large until you compare it with [relevant baseline]. On that basis, [calibrated conclusion].`

### Probability update

`New [data] moves my view from roughly [old] to [new]. The change comes mostly from [driver], not [popular explanation].`

### Conditional forecast

`If [claim] is right, we should soon observe [Y] and [Z]. If those fail to appear, the claim needs updating.`

### Incentive reveal

`This looks like a disagreement about [stated value]. It may be better explained by [incentive structure].`

### Data puzzle

`Which group is actually most likely to [behavior]? The answer is [group], and the cross-tab explains why.`

### Update ledger

`What changed my mind: [evidence]. What didn't: [stable evidence]. What would change it next: [trigger].`

## Quote-post template

1. Preserve the source's necessary context.
2. Identify the missing variable or incorrect comparison.
3. Add one calibrated conclusion.

`Useful data, but [missing condition]. Compared with [baseline], this looks more like [interpretation] than [popular claim].`

## Short analytical post

```text
[Sharp conclusion or puzzle]

[One decisive number or comparison.]

[Condition/exception.] The mechanism is probably [incentive or causal explanation].

I would update this view if [observable trigger].
```

## Mini Model Talk

```text
Current estimate: [X]
Previous estimate: [Y]
Main driver: [A]
Largest unknown: [B]
What would change the estimate: [C]
```

## Postmortem

```text

```

## Voice rules

- Lead with the result, not throat-clearing.
- Use conversational analytical language, not academic cosplay.
- Use one strong adjective only when the evidence supports it.
- Prefer `probably`, `roughly`, and `the evidence suggests` to fake precision.
- Avoid generic slogans, moral grandstanding, and certainty theater.
- Use parentheses or asides sparingly to acknowledge nuance.
- End with a mechanism, implication, or update condition rather than engagement bait.
