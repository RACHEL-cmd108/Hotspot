# Length Routing and Templates

## The Governing Rule

Make length an output of the evidence chain. Do not begin by deciding to write a thread.

Ask:

1. What is the single new fact?
2. How many pieces of evidence must a skeptical reader inspect?
3. Does the mechanism require explanation?
4. How costly is being wrong?
5. How many plausible counterarguments exist?

Use the shortest form that answers all five without hiding a material caveat.

Apply this routing only after the publish gate. Gate score answers whether the event merits attention; evidence shape answers how long the content should be. A high gate score can still produce a short signal when the verified claim is narrow.

## Reply

Use when the parent post supplies nearly all context.

Structure:

`specific correction or second-order implication → one number/rule → optional dry punchline`

Do not turn a reply into an orphaned mini-essay. Promote it to a standalone post when readers need missing context.

## Quote Post

Use when the quoted source is credible and the additional value is one layer deeper.

Structure:

`what the source shows → what the market/rule gets wrong → consequence`

Avoid quoting a weak source merely to borrow reach.

## Short Post

Use for one anomaly or decisive update.

Structure:

`strange event/actor → exact price or money → stance/action → uncertainty or self-aware punchline`

Hook families:

- “Small detail, large mismatch”: a minor fact contradicts the price.
- “Money makes it real”: disclose the stake or P&L consequence.
- “Correct but loses”: expose a resolution paradox.
- “Character enters”: give a recurring market actor a memorable neutral label.
- “Before the news”: flag suspicious timing without alleging identity or wrongdoing.
- “Blunt verdict”: state a defensible conclusion followed by the decisive number.

## Medium Post

Use when one mechanism must be taught.

Structure:

1. Strong verdict.
2. Price, chart, rule, or primary fact.
3. Overlooked mechanism.
4. Own position or action.
5. Strongest caveat and update condition.

Keep one thesis. If two independent mechanisms are necessary, consider a long case file.

## Long Case File

Use when readers must reconstruct the event to evaluate the conclusion.

Structure:

1. Name the case with an original, memorable label.
2. Offer a clear opt-out: explain that detail follows.
3. Reconstruct the timeline.
4. Quote or paraphrase the decisive rule and official source.
5. Show price, position, volume, wallet, or other evidence.
6. Explain incentives and mechanism.
7. Steelman the strongest alternative explanation.
8. Deliver the verdict with calibrated confidence.
9. Recommend an actionable platform, rule, or process fix.
10. State the next fact that would change the verdict.

Do not use length as a substitute for evidence.

## Postmortem

Structure:

1. Original thesis and timestamp.
2. Entry price, position, and exposure.
3. What happened.
4. What was predicted correctly.
5. What was wrong or lucky.
6. P&L or non-financial outcome.
7. Process change for the next trade or post.

Postmortems build credibility only when losses and errors receive the same scrutiny as wins.

## Escalation Example

An unexplained price move begins as a monitored alert. A confirmed large order makes it a short signal. Rule ambiguity plus a public statement makes it a medium explanation. Multiple wallets, a disputed timeline, and platform governance consequences justify a long case file. Settlement creates the postmortem.
